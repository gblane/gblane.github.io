  Design — Section 2 of 5: Physics & colour pipeline

  Slab model. Pure Beer–Lambert, per your spec: T(λ) = exp(−μₐ(λ)·L), with μₐ(λ)
  = Σᵢ εᵢ(λ)·cᵢ plus the Jacques melanin power law. No scattering, no Fresnel
  reflection at the faces, no fluorescence — stated explicitly in the About card
  so nobody reads the swatch as a real cuvette measurement.

  The one real correctness trap. Your tissue data is base-e μₐ in mm⁻¹.
  PhotochemCAD and essentially all chemistry sources give molar absorptivity in
  M⁻¹cm⁻¹, which is base-10. Mixing them without converting is a silent
  factor-of-2.303 (plus a factor of 10 for cm→mm):

  $$\mu_a,[\mathrm{mm^{-1}}] =
  \ln(10)\cdot\varepsilon_{10},[\mathrm{M^{-1}cm^{-1}}]\cdot c,[\mathrm{M}] /
  10$$

  Every library entry declares its native units and converts on load, so the
  internal representation is uniformly base-e mm⁻¹. This gets a test assertion.

  Colour chain. Both swatches share one pipeline and differ by exactly one step:

  1. Φ(λ) = S(λ)·T(λ) — transmitted spectrum
  2. k = 100 / ∫S(λ)ȳ(λ)dλ — normalise so a clear slab has Y=100
  3. X = k∫Φx̄dλ, likewise Y, Z — CIE 1931 2° observer, 380–780 nm at 1 nm
  4. Adapted view only: Bradford chromatic adaptation transform, source white →
  D65
  5. Lab relative to D65; XYZ → linear sRGB → gamma → hex

  Framing the difference as "adaptation on/off" rather than "different white
  points" keeps both views D65-referenced and makes the pipeline one code path
  with a flag. It also buys two free invariants for test.html: a clear slab in
  the adapted view must give exactly L*=100, a*=b*=0, #ffffff under any
  illuminant; and when the source is D65 the two swatches must agree to floating
  point.

  Brightness is preserved, not renormalised. A dense slab goes genuinely dark —
  that is Beer–Lambert doing its job, and the L* readout makes it quantitative.
  Saturated dyes will land outside sRGB; I'll clip to [0,1] and surface an
  "outside sRGB gamut" flag when clipping occurs, rather than clipping silently.

  Does Section 2 look right? Sections 3–5 are controls/plots, data sourcing, and
  error handling + testing.
